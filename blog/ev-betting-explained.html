+EV Betting Explained: How to Actually Beat the Bookies

Most punters lose because they bet on who they think will win. Winning punters bet on value — and over enough bets, that difference is everything.

What does +EV actually mean?

EV stands for expected value — the average result you'd get if you placed the same bet thousands of times. A bet is +EV (positive expected value) when the odds on offer are bigger than the true probability of it happening. That gap is your edge.

Example: a coin flip is 50% to land heads, so "fair" odds are $2.00. If a bookie offered you $2.10 on heads, that's +EV — you'd make money long-term even though you'll lose plenty of individual flips.

How do you find value?

You need an estimate of the true probability that's better than the bookmaker's. Two common approaches:

  • Consensus de-vig: compare the odds across many bookmakers, strip out their margin ("the vig"), and you get a sharp estimate of true probability. If one book is offering better than that, it's value.
  • A predictive model: build your own probability from data (team ratings, player form) and bet when the market disagrees with it.

At Bettr Punters we use both, depending on the market — de-vig for efficient game lines, and a trained model for sports like the NBL.

Why losses are normal (and fine)

This is the part that breaks most people. A +EV bettor can lose 5 in a row and still be winning long-term. Variance is brutal in the short run. The only way to know if you have a real edge is to track every bet and look at the ROI over hundreds of them — not cherry-picked wins.

That's exactly why our entire record is public, wins and losses included. If the edge is real, the numbers show it over time.

The takeaway

Stop chasing winners. Chase value, stake sensibly, track everything, and let the maths play out. That's the whole game.
