AFL Betting: Why Tracked Records Matter (and How to Read ROI)

Anyone can post a winning bet slip. The question is: what did they post before the game, and what's the record once you count the losses?

The screenshot problem

Most betting "tipsters" market with screenshots of big wins. It's meaningless — you never see the losing slips, the deleted tips, or the bets that were edited after the result. A single green slip proves nothing.

What a real record looks like

A credible tipster logs every pick the moment it's issued, at the odds available then, and grades it automatically against the real result — wins and losses. That's the only honest way to prove an edge.

How to read the numbers

  • ROI (return on investment): profit divided by total staked. +5% to +10% over a big sample is genuinely strong. Anyone claiming +50% long-term is either tiny-sample or lying.
  • Units: profit measured in bet-sizes, not dollars — so it's comparable regardless of bankroll.
  • Sample size: 20 bets means nothing. Hundreds start to matter. Variance needs time to wash out.
  • Hit rate vs ROI: a 40% hit rate can be very profitable if the winners are at big odds. Don't judge on strike rate alone.
